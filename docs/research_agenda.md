# Exploratory biogeography and the next biological questions

This module describes the wider research program as of 15 October 2026. It is separate from the climate-representation manuscript. The public table replay does not reproduce these geographical or historical analyses, and this snapshot does not claim to have discovered new *Spea* population divisions.

## What has been useful so far

| Question examined | Current result in the working archive | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Western/interior intermontana contrast | The selected Onaqui–Uinta environmental-cost crossing was less constrained than its matched western control. Some circuit orderings changed with grain. | Range-wide isolation, actual permeability and an ancestry boundary |
| Western basin chain | The selected upper Owens–Bridgeport crossing was not unusually constrained relative to the northern control. | The much longer Harney–Inyo axis, individual ridges and historical isolation |
| Basin and Range versus Colorado Plateaus climate response | Adding provincial climate terms beyond a specified signed-distance climate-response spline changed the primary mean by −0.00990 nats, positive in one of three supported folds. | Ecological equivalence, geography beyond that one distance coordinate, and genetic subdivision |
| Great Basin paleolakes | Fixed maximum/Provo outlines changed geometric detours while retaining connections for the selected pairs. | Dated ecological routes, changing breeding habitat and successful dispersal |
| Northern range / British Columbia | Independent ice chronology constrains physical opening; nonanalog historical climate limits a climatic colonization interpretation. | Actual arrival time, source populations and one versus multiple colonization histories |
| Other small vertebrates | A bounded review of frogs, toads and lizards retained both regional subdivision and northern continuity as useful comparisons. | Whether *Spea* shares any particular boundary or demographic history |

The cost surfaces transform fitted environmental recording patterns into an assumed movement cost. They are not calibrated dispersal models. Local negative comparisons narrow particular hypotheses; they cannot rule out isolation elsewhere or in the past. Historical geometry, climate suitability, movement and ancestry require distinct evidence.

The provincial comparison used nine fixed fits in the three folds meeting prespecified common-climate support requirements. Two other folds lacked that support and were not fitted. Its small negative mean increment is conditional predictive evidence, not a test establishing niche equivalence or a definitive model of geographical variation.

## Priorities that would change the interpretation

The most consequential next evidence is independent, replicated population sampling across the proposed contrasts, including southeastern Oregon/northwestern Great Basin contact versus continuity and western/eastern basin histories. Existing reference coverage is geographically uneven and cannot test all proposed divisions.

Breeding-site distribution and hydroperiod are also plausible missing ecological dimensions. Their value should be tested with observations matched to that question; poor climate-model transfer alone does not identify ponds, predators or physiology as the cause. Contemporary pond information would not automatically transfer to the Last Glacial Maximum.

A stronger predictive result would use a genuinely fresh, locked evaluation after methodological choices are fixed. Repeating predictor, background or radius searches on the same inspected geographical folds cannot supply independent confirmation. Better population or habitat observations may be more informative than another sweep of those models.

## Scope of the contribution

The public climate study contributes a bounded empirical comparison: explicit simple controls, geographical allocation scores, retained adverse outcomes, and sensitivity to fitting and weighting. The exploratory biogeography work contributes candidate tests and constraints on interpretation. These are useful intermediate results in a living research project; neither component currently establishes adaptation, a new lineage, or a dated dispersal history.

The biological context for the northern/southern hammondii operational units is [Neal, Johnson and Shaffer (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10592-018-1066-7). Independent physiological context includes [Gerick et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.12261) and [Greenberg and Palen (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2020.2273); their measured life stages and outcomes differ from the recording-density target used here.
